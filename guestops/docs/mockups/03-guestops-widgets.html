GuestOps widgets — the five frames, drawn late and offered as one audit

Read this before approving anything below. These five widgets were built before they were drawn. That is the gap: every other GuestOps surface went through a frame the owner audited, and the widgets went through design page 56's mechanism and the SDK's contract instead — both of which they satisfy, and neither of which is a drawing anybody looked at.

So these frames are a proposal made after the fact, and the honest risk is circularity: drawn from what exists, they invite approval of what exists. They are offered anyway because the alternative — drawing five fictions and then reconciling — audits nothing real. If a frame here is wrong, the build changes. The frames are the specification from the moment they are approved, exactly as the seventeen are.
The canvas is 320 × 384 and the shell does not resize to content (page 56). A widget that could size itself would make the dock's popovers ragged. So content that does not fit is cut by the widget: every frame below shows a fixed number of rows because that is what fits, never because a query returned that many.

Three rules every frame obeys. No writes — a widget is a question answered, and v1 takes no action. No number the backend cannot honestly compute — absent, never approximate. And touching an answer opens the application at the screen showing the same rows.

Nothing is normalised
Channel and market code are shown in the source's own words. The segment every hotel reports on is only reportable if it survives the journey unaltered.
Three of each, and the labels say so. The canvas holds six rows and two labels; a seventh row would be drawn and then cut by overflow:hidden, which is a row a person cannot see and cannot know is missing. The cut is stated on each label rather than in a footnote under both, so it is read on the list it applies to. Two lists rather than one, because a channel and a market code are different questions a revenue manager asks in the same breath — where the booking came from, and what kind of business it is. Folding them into one segment is exactly the normalisation the reference system did, and it is why its own reports could not be reconciled with the PMS's.
What these five deliberately do not do. No writes — v1 widgets take no action: no check-in, no approve, no assign (page 56). No number the backend cannot honestly compute — a row the domain cannot answer is not drawn at all, which is why the contact never appears on any of them (GUEST-Q12) and why free is a remainder rather than a stored figure. No sizing — the frame is the guaranteed size and the widget does the cutting. And no second opinion: every count here is the same number the application's own screens carry, read from the same projection, so a widget and a screen cannot disagree about the day.
