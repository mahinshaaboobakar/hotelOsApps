GuestOps — the gold design, for the owner's approval. The reservation book: bookings are groups, the room-stay is the anchor, and every operation happens to a stay. Four marks carry the whole application — from Opera for a fact the PMS wrote, override for a staff write on a PMS-managed stay, disagrees where the two differ, and missing for what a record honestly lacks. Frames are the desktop window at 1220 wide; rail, sheet, dialog and confirmation rule are the platform's (ADR 0106), unchanged. Every frame cites the scenario it draws — Sn from 01-the-front-desk-scenarios.md.

S33 and S35, drawn as one screen. The rule the whole PMS mode rests on: one truth leaves the application — the standing override is what the board shows, what Room Care hears and what Context resolves; the disagreement is a flag on that answer, never a second answer (GUEST-Q3). A recorded override is a person looking at the guest; the inbound fact is automation and possibly stale. Clearing is the stay's write permission — the same permission that made the override — with two named values and both kept in history; there is no separate permission and no supervisor escalation, because that would re-introduce what the ruling refused. Read-from-PMS versus staff-editable is a mark, not a lock: FROM OPERA says who established the value, and staff may still write it — PMS-connected has never meant read-only (GUEST-Q1, amended). Every timestamp carries its basis — OBSERVED is the desk, DERIVED FROM PROPERTY CLOCK is a date plus the property's check-out time (R12, R13, S22).

S36 and GUEST-Q4 — the fork was removed, not chosen. The property is PMS-writes-first at all times, so an override means one thing in every condition and the screen always says the same true thing: your action stands. A mode switch keyed on connector health would flip the desk's meaning mid-shift on a false trigger — and connector health is exactly the signal R27 proved unreliable, since a connector can be authenticated, polling and green while check-ins specifically have stopped. Four rows, four rulings. The candidate link is staff-confirmed and never automatic (GUEST-Q5) — the reference joined stays by matching (companyId, siteId, surname, firstName, arrivalDate) against its own private copy, and a wrong match silently merges two guests' histories, which is worse than a duplicate. GuestOps's first day is not an empty book. The Integration Hub has been normalising reservation and guest facts since the connector shipped and holding them deferred — with their business date and provenance — precisely because this domain did not exist to own them (ADR 0128; connector brief §12). Installing this application is what turns that queue on, and the replay is idempotent by construction: a fact that has already been applied changes nothing and publishes nothing. The empty state that would be wrong here is the usual one — "no reservations yet, create your first booking" — on a property with two thousand of them waiting.

Settlement happens in Opera. In a standalone property it happens nowhere in v1 — a consequence accepted knowingly with GUEST-Q6, which is why the first deployments are PMS-connected.
Why this frame exists at all. A gold mockup that quietly omitted the balance would leave the owner approving a check-out screen that a receptionist cannot actually finish, and the omission would be discovered in the build. Drawing it with the caveat attached is the same treatment the connector round gave CONN-Q9: the frame is shown, the gap is named, and nothing is built behind it until someone rules. What is in v1 is the stay's commercial terms — the rate, the guarantee, the cancellation penalty as an offset rather than a resolved deadline (R18), and every amount with its currency and its tax basis (R19) — which is frame 2's panel, and is the part that would otherwise be lost the day it arrives from Opera.
What this mockup does not draw. No folio posting, payment or invoice (GUEST-Q6 — Finance's). No guest merge, no person-graph, no "same guest, third stay" panel — that sentence is Guest360's answer surfaced here, and Guest360 does not exist yet (G360-Q1). No write-back to Opera anywhere: nothing on any screen reaches the PMS in v1 (CONN-Q5). No room state — Room Care owns clean and ready; these screens announce occupancy and assert nothing about cleaning (S21). No mode switch, because there is no second mode (GUEST-Q4). Every frame above traces to a scenario, and frame 8 is the only one drawing something unruled — deliberately, and marked.
