GuestOps — the gold design, for the owner's approval. The reservation book: bookings are groups, the room-stay is the anchor, and every operation happens to a stay. Four marks carry the whole application — from Opera for a fact the PMS wrote, override for a staff write on a PMS-managed stay, disagrees where the two differ, and missing for what a record honestly lacks. A fifth, another app, marks anything on these screens that GuestOps does not own and reads through the Context Service. Frames are the desktop window at 1220 wide; rail, sheet, dialog and confirmation rule are the platform's (ADR 0106), unchanged. Every frame cites the scenario it draws — Sn from 01-the-front-desk-scenarios.md.

The list is where a guest at the counter is found, so the search covers what a guest can actually say: their name, the number they booked with, or the confirmation number Opera gave them. A booking's row shows its stays, not a room — BK-4471 says 1 of 3 known and BK-4506 says 2, because a booking is a group and the room is a fact about a stay inside it (GUEST-Q2). Cancelled and no-show rows stay in the list: a cancelled reservation exists, its penalty may be chargeable, and a no-show is reportable — neither is a deletion (S25, S27, ADR 0062). Waitlisted is shown as waitlisted (GUEST-Q9): a first-class state in every major PMS, and the desk must be able to see it as one rather than as a confirmed booking. It holds no room — a waitlist is a queue position, so counting it against inventory would make a full hotel look oversold and hide the room a cancellation gives back. The platform now carries both patterns in common.v1 and one test picks between them — is the count a fact, or a moving target? A property's bookings are bounded and countable, so 218 is a fact and showing 1–8 of 218 is something the wire can actually answer. The cursor is kept for feeds that rotate while they are read, where an offset would skip rather than merely mislead — the Logs pane is the worked case, not this list.

This is the screen the owner asked for, and what it deliberately is not. It is a property capability, not a country's law compiled into the product: a property that has no reporting obligation turns it off and never sees the tab, the flag or the list. The deadline is an offset — 24 hours after arrival — for the same reason a cancellation deadline is (R18): move the arrival and the deadline moves with it, while a stored date silently stops matching. Configuration is the application's own — an application is a bundle of UI, backend, schema, permissions, events and configuration (ADR 0051) — and none of it belongs in Master Data, because none of it describes what the property is. And the one honest limit is stated on the screen itself: HotelOS does not send anything. An automatic filing is an outbound connector, which the platform has never built and which v1's inbound-only connector contract excludes — reported rather than drawn as a button that would not work. Ruled 2026-08-31 and recorded on the outbound round's own row: a statutory filing is a distinct capability class — a legal assertion, no silent retry, and the receipt is part of the record. Which is why reference is on this screen today: a person files and records the receipt now, the connector files and records the same receipt later, on the same row.
What this mockup does not draw, and why. No folio posting, payment or invoice — GUEST-Q6 puts them in Finance's later round; frame 7's second band shows exactly where the line falls and is reported as a finding rather than proposed. No guest merge, no person-graph, no "same guest, third stay" panel — that sentence is Guest360's answer surfaced here, and Guest360 does not exist yet (G360-Q1). No write-back to Opera anywhere: nothing on any screen reaches the PMS in v1 (CONN-Q5), and frame 8's cancellation dialog says so out loud rather than leaving it to be discovered. No room state and no cleaning decisions — Room Care owns clean, ready and who cleans; frame 6 reports its record and asserts nothing (APPS-Q1, S21). No job assignment — Jobs owns work; frame 5 hands over a request and stops. No mode switch, because there is no second mode (GUEST-Q4). Every frame traces to a scenario; the two cross-application tabs (5, 6) and frame 7's second band are the only places drawing something the platform cannot do today, and each is marked where it is drawn.
