Teams — drawn after the build, for the owner's audit. Nothing here is approved.

Why this page exists. The team object was ruled and built on 2026-09-04 from Jobs' S3-D1 — "a named group of posted staff within a department" — and it went in without a drawn screen. That is the gap this closes. Every frame below is what the built service already does and nothing more: form, rename, stand down and back up, add and remove members, list, and who was in a team on a given day. Where a frame shows a rule, the rule is in the code and its test, not in the picture. Frame 6 is the one to look at hardest — it is a consequence the build has and the interface does not admit to.

A team belongs to one department. Assignment routing is departmental — a job's pool, its policy and its accountability are all per department — so a team spanning two would make which pool does this sit in unanswerable.
What the service already answers. The list is per department or all, and stood-down teams are hidden unless asked for. Sorted by department then name, which is the order the service returns rather than a choice the screen makes. The member count is the live count on the day being viewed: a membership is effective-dated, so Pool Bar has 0 is a fact about today rather than a team that was never used. Two live teams in one department may not share a name — enforced by a unique index as well as by the service, because the service's check and a concurrent insert can both pass, and a supervisor picking "Team A" from two identical rows is choosing at random.

The date in the header is what "members" means. A membership is effective-dated, so this reads who was in this team on that day — the question a report asks about March, answered by the same screen.
Names are borrowed, never held. Workforce stores a staff_id and nothing about the person — its proto says so in its own header — so every name here is read from Master Data at the moment the answer is composed and kept nowhere. That is the serving is not storing ruling, and it is why this screen costs one lookup for all six rather than six. A name Master Data does not answer for stays blank rather than becoming Unknown: "we could not find this person" and "this person has no name" are different facts, and neither is a placeholder this application gets to invent.

Deactivate and Reactivate, never Archive and Restore — ADR 0062's vocabulary, and reactivation is required rather than optional: a deactivate with no counterpart states a capability in the schema and withholds it from the service. The frame to audit hardest, and it is a finding rather than a feature. The build already does this: ending a posting closes every team membership it supported, in the posting's own transaction, because a membership left standing routes work to somebody who left the department last month with nothing anywhere saying so. What the build does not do is tell anyone. A supervisor ending a posting empties two teams and sees no mention of it. 8 · The shift fan-out — no screen, and that is the design

Built in the same round, and deliberately invisible here. shift.started and shift.ended are announced by a sweep as each boundary falls, so every application that needs to know whether a department is staffed hears it. There is no Workforce screen for that, and inventing one would be inventing a job for a person who has none — nobody watches a fan-out.

Where it would be visible if you want it is Operations Center, which exists precisely for is the platform well: the last tick, the boundaries announced today, anything outstanding. That is a different application's surface and a different stream's, and it is not proposed here.

What can already be seen from Workforce is the Shift Board widget: who is on now, by department, from the same coverage logic the announcement uses. When a manager asks "did Housekeeping's shift change go through?", the board is the answer they actually want.
What is drawn here and NOT built — three things, so the audit is not misread:

1 · The Teams rail entry and every screen above. The service exists; no UI module has been written for it. The module has eight screens and this would be a ninth.
2 · Frame 5's "keep its members" toggle. Standing a team down leaves memberships alone today. The switch shows that; its other position is a decision nobody has made.
3 · Frame 6's consequence panel. The behaviour is built and tested; the telling is not. It is the one I would fix first.
