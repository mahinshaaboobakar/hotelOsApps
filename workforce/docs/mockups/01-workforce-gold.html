Workforce — the gold design, revision 2, for the owner's read. Revision 1 was drawn from a chapter written before the owner had been asked anything. Eight subjects of the comparison study and the rulings WF-Q1–Q16 later, six frames change and four surfaces are new. Three marks the app is built from, and two of them have changed: a shift chip that renders from the property's own catalogue — its colour, its short code (M A SB) — a struck day for leave, and the MOD duty, now drawn as a span that crosses midnight rather than a name in a day cell. Frames are the desktop window at 1220 wide; rail, sheet, dialog and the confirmation rule are the platform's (ADR 0106), unchanged.
What changed, and what ruled it. Frame 2 — the chip is data, not three hardcoded classes (WF-Q11, N2); the MOD ribbon is a timeline (WF-Q8); the zone appears (WF-Q7). Frame 1 gains its first action (WF-Q9). Frame 4 becomes two kinds of queue. Frame 5 stops being a month of day cells. Frame 6 gains the zone. Frames 7–11 are new: attendance (WF-Q10), the property's workforce policy (WF-Q11/Q13/Q14), creating a shift (owner, 2026-08-31 — the catalogue had no page of its own), the month-end sheet (§3.7), and the printed week (N1, SHELL-Q23). Frame 1 was renamed and re-aimed on the owner's reading that Workforce is not accessed by every staff member — a scope correction rather than a label, and the closing note reports what it moves. Nothing here is drawn on a guess: every frame's caption names the ruling it implements.

Warn, never block — and the frame is drawn against the negative it permits. WF-Q5 is warn-and-allow, and a ledger debited on approval means an overdrawn balance is a real state, not an error: it reads −1 of 15, approved overdraw, in amber, and the request sheet says plainly that the request can still be made. Revision 1 drew the balance as a plain positive number and had never been tested against a minus sign. The balance is a ledger: accrual credits it, approval debits it, cancelling an approved request credits it back — the row at the bottom says so, because a debit with no matching credit turns a cancellation into a silent forfeit.
