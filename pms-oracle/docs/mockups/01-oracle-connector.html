Oracle connector — operational surfaces

pms-oracle/docs/mockups/01-oracle-connector.html · 2026-08-31 · Stream DD. The companion to docs/chapters/03-the-oracle-connector-design.md, and the second half of the owner's gate.

What this is not. Not a second copy of the platform frames. docs/working/42-the-connector-round.html already draws installing, configuring and observing any connector. This page draws only what is Oracle's — the five things a support engineer needs when somebody says “housekeeping never heard about 402”, and the one artefact a hotel's OPERA operator has to be handed.

Where these render. Five of the six sit in Operations Center → Integrations as connector-specific detail — ADR 0073, operators observe. The setup sheet is an administrator's artefact and comes from Software Center's Integration Manager (18A:559, ADR 0091), behind the Copy setup sheet button the platform mockup's frame 4 already shows.

And the open mechanism, again. “OHIP queue depth” and “join backlog” are Oracle's concepts, not the platform's. Whether the Hub renders them generically from declared per-integration diagnostics, or the connector ships a ui.module with its own views, is CONN-Q9's observational half — the same open question as the configuration form. These frames draw the content, not the mechanism, and each carries the tag content, not mechanism where it matters.

Why 205 and 206 differ
Identical on all four axes — occupied, dirty, due out. 205 is next sold in three days; 206 is sold again tonight. Strip the linen in 205; make 206 up for the next guest.

The deciding field is nextBlockedAt — when the room is next sold — which exists only in the PMS's inventory and can be derived from no current status. Requirement R3.

The HK-department column is empty on purpose. The web flavour does not send it; the cloud flavour does. An axis a source does not supply is drawn absent rather than filled with a default — this screen shows what Oracle said, not what we assumed.
Pseudo rooms are not here. House and group accounts arrive room-shaped and are excluded before this view — they are not physical rooms, and mapping one to a canonical room would be a permanent error (R4). They appear in the platform mockup's unmappable queue, where an operator marks them.

Three things this trace makes checkable rather than assumed. The timestamp is marked expected, not observed — OHIP gave an expected arrival time for a stay already in house, and a report built on it measures the reservation rather than the guest. The amount carries net of tax, because Oracle sends amountBeforeTax and another vendor sends gross into the same field. And business_date shows the input and the boundary, not just the answer.
Replay runs from line one. The stored bytes, not the PMS — which for this integration is the only option, since the queue that delivered the notification no longer holds it.

We pair "Checked In" (contact details) with "CHECKED IN" (room) on surname, first name and arrival date. A stay is not published until both halves arrive.
The secret is shown once, on this sheet. It is written to the Token Vault when the integration is saved and is never displayed again — this sheet is the only moment it is legible, which is why the sheet is generated on demand rather than stored.
Section 5 exists because of a defect in the reference. Its downstream treated a read timeout as success, because it had no way to know whether a slow call had landed. Telling the sender plainly that 202 means stored, that re-sending is safe, and that we de-duplicate, removes the guess at the other end of the wire rather than only at ours.
content, not mechanism Whether this sheet is rendered by Software Center from the connector's declaration or by a connector-supplied view is CONN-Q9. Its content is the connector's either way — only pms-oracle knows that this PMS sends two half-messages for one check-in.

Deliberately not drawn.

Anything the platform mockup already draws — install, configure, credentials, health, the inbox pipeline, the unmappable queue, replay. This page is only what is Oracle's; docs/working/42-the-connector-round.html is the rest.
Write-back surfaces — the housekeeping status push OHIP would accept is a later capability (ADR 0128 §4, CONN-Q5), so there is no screen for it here and no disabled control pretending there might be.
Guarantee, deposit and cancellation terms — OHIP supplies them (R18) and their owning domain does not exist yet, so they are carried on the fact and have no operational surface until Reservations/GuestOps ships.
The cloud flavour's setup sheet — there is nothing to hand anyone: we dial out, and the credential is entered on our side.
Who renders any of this — CONN-Q9, tagged on the three frames where it bites.
