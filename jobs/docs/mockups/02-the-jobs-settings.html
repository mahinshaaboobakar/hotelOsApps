LOCKED by the owner, 2026-09-04, after five redline passes the same day. These frames are the ones the build is audited against, frame beside capture. A change from here is a new redline, recorded on the page, never a silent edit.

Companion to 01-the-jobs-screens.html, split out on the owner's leave (2026-09-04) when the concern-policy flow made one page too long. Same palette authority — the ADR 0106 tokens, no hex of its own — and the same chrome. Dark frames first.

Times: every timestamp is a date and a time. The form — 24-hour or AM/PM, day-month or month-day — comes from the property, read through Context; Jobs configures none of it. Corrected 2026-09-04: the property carries timezone and locale (ADR 0075, CORE-Q16, ADR 0123) and no explicit hour-format setting; Jobs renders from locale until the architect rules whether Core adds one — chapter §9 carries the question.

Part A is the Settings tab of the app, one frame per sub-tab, each with its purpose stated. Part B is the policies list for two departments, the three-step flow that creates one — scope picked as department › category › item — and the table of how a job finds its policy.

Redline folded (2026-09-04, fourth pass) — one department, different promises per category. Housekeeping › Cleaning in 30 minutes but › Bottle of water in 10: the "due within" of a job is not a department number. It resolves item → category → department → property (chapter §2.3): the catalogue item's own due-within first (Deep clean 90 min), else its category's (Bottle of water 10 min, Cleaning 30 min), else the department's row here, else the property's. The same chain picks the concern policy — at-risk point, stuck tests, ladder — so a category can carry its own ladder too (a guest-request category may reach the manager where a cleaning category never does). The scope rail on the left is that chain, and a category or item row is added from the catalogue page (frame 7 of page 01: "Due within", and "Concern policy" beside it), not here.
Purpose: this is the whole escalation design (S5 D1–D3) as one table — how long a job of each priority has, when it turns AT_RISK, what counts as STUCK, who becomes accountable at each step, and whether a manager hears of a P1 already at risk (the owner's flexibility ask). Table concern_policy; property default with per-department override. Everything the 60-second sweep decides, it decides from this table.

Draws S5 D2–D3 and §6's sweep. A ladder is an ordered list of roles, not people — assignee, department supervisor, department manager, property jobs manager, GM — each with the concern state and delay at which it becomes accountable. Roles resolve to today's people through Context (Workforce postings and headships; the Identity grant), so a roster change never edits a policy. Per priority, because a P3 need not reach a manager. The "manager from at risk" toggle is the owner's ask, per priority.
Redline folded (2026-09-04, third pass). "How do we set the ladder" — drawn as the third step of creating a policy: roles in order, each with its trigger, an add-step dialog, drag to reorder. Saving writes one concern_policy row with its ladder rows; the sweep uses it from the next minute.
